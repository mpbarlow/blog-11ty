Apple used to get a lot of shit for “thin & light”. Some of it was warranted: the butterfly keyboard was a “thin & light” thing, as was Apple’s first foldable—the iPhone 6 (though I still maintain that you shouldn’t sit on your phone if you don’t want it to bend). Battery life and thermals were sometimes compromised.

Critics (by which I mean: people on forums) argued that there was nothing inherently virtuous about thinness or lightness, and that a more utilitarian approach should be taken, never sacrificing function for form. I think they were wrong.

I recently bought a couple of iPods off eBay: a white 4GB 1st gen and a black 8GB 2nd gen. Mostly out of novelty and nostalgia than any intent to seriously use them, though I love a single-purpose device and I would like to find a way to keep them in active use.

I never owned either of these when they were new—my first iPod was the stubby 3rd gen nano, followed later by an iPod touch when I couldn’t afford an iPhone, and finally an iPod Classic as my collection outgrew the flash-based offerings of the time—but I always thought they were peak iPod form-factor and some of my favourite industrial design to ever come out of Cupertino.

They’re very cheap to buy now, mostly because they’re almost all battered and have dead or dying batteries, but I’ve managed to find two pretty good examples. The white one has some light scuffs and scratches; the black one was advertised as being kept in a case its whole life and indeed is in spectacularly good condition.

Their batteries seem okay, with both managing the better part of a week so far in sleep mode with some occasional fiddling. The meters jump all over the place, but from what I remember these had quite coarse readings (in 25% increments) and so I hope that with a couple of full discharge cycles I can nurse them back to serviceable health. (One caveat I’ll admit as a failure of “thin & light” is that these things are hell to repair and service, though I think that was more a cost and convenience choice on Apple’s part than a physical limitation.)

These things are an utter _delight_. Even just as physical objects they are lovely to hold and play around with. Despite being 21 and 20 years old, respectively, they still feel marvels of engineering. I mean, just look at this:

It’s barely there. It weighs practically nothing and takes up almost no space. You could keep this in your pocket for the rest of your life, ready to serve up ~1500 of your favourite songs whenever you like, and never feel encumbered by it. It has no algorithmic feeds, offers no subscriptions, and never demands your attention. It just does one thing, really well (though you can also play Breakout or Solitaire if you like). Meanwhile, my iPhone 18 Pro—which I like many aspects of!—has literally worn holes in two pairs of jeans, and increasingly feels like a physical manifestation of all the negative aspects of modern online life. A not-so-metaphorical brick weighing me down.

## What’s a computer?

Getting these set up on a modern Mac was an…interesting experience. The iTunes Store still just about exists in Music.app, and the syncing functionality was moved to the Finder some time ago. However, it’s clear none of this stuff has been touched in a long while. Restoring iPod firmware seems to have been broken since macOS Monterey from what I saw online, and involves unplugging the iPod at a particular point in the process, then rebooting the entire Mac to get convince Finder to see it again once the restore completes. Many people actually recommend just using Windows, where the final build of iTunes is still usable.

Once that’s done, you then need to get your purchased music downloaded in a format the iPod can work with. For reasons I’ve long since forgotten, I replaced all my iTunes music with the Apple Music versions years ago. I think I was maybe trying to prevent the iTunes Match-era behaviour where albums would randomly get split in two or tracks replaced with compilation album versions (unsuccessfully by the way—that still happens to this day). The only way I found to get the purchased version was to delete the album from my library, go to the (incredibly slow) iTunes Store purchases page, then redownload it from there. While you can see that you’ve purchased an album from its Store page, you can’t download it from there 🤷‍♂️.

You also need to make sure that you limit your download quality to 256kbps AAC—while iTunes purchases grant you the lossless version if one has since become available, they download as DRM-encumbered .movpkg files the iPod can neither see nor play. To add some extra fun, if you forget to do this, and therefore have to delete and re-add the albums _again_, you will find that deleting the purchased album from your library has moved it to “Hidden Purchases”. These must then be unhidden, track-by-track, from Account > Account Settings > Hidden Purchases in Music.app. This menu is also quite broken; I had to load it up three or four times to get it to list all the tracks that were actually hidden.

## Today’s devices

Apple can still do “thin & light”, it’s just rarely at the top of the marketing sheet anymore. The 11” iPad Pro is so thin and so light that merely attaching the standard folio case adds 50% to both the thickness and weight. Every time I pick up my wife’s MacBook Air I wonder whether it wasn’t a waste buying a MacBook Pro. The Mac mini is a spectacular little computer, even though it has no real portability requirements.

The iPhone Air is an interesting proposition, as it seems to offer exactly what I’m asking for—“thin & light”, in exchange for some utility in the form of battery life and camera quality. Indeed, if there’s an Air 2 in the spring I may be tempted to swap my 18 Pro for it, even though I would sorely miss the telephoto lens.

The problem with the Air is that it only offers thin & light in a very relative sense. It’s larger in width and height than even my 18 Pro, it’s heavier than an iPhone 6, and while it is—for about 85% of it—“the thinnest iPhone ever”, it’s still got that huge hump on the back.

My favourite ever phone was my 12 mini. The battery life was shitty and the camera wasn’t all that, but it was a phone that was easy to forget about, and I mean that in the best possible way. I want something like that again.
